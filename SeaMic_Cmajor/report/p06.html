2.5 Soft gating vs. hard muting

A hard gate (multiply by 0 below threshold) creates discontinuities — clicks — and interacts badly with Opus discontinuous transmission (DTX): the encoder's comfort-noise generator expects a quieted signal, not digital silence, and a hard edge produces spectral splatter. A downward expander applied after the gain stage attenuates toward a floor (here a maximum of −10 dB) with a fast 10 ms close and 150 ms release. The residual background below the gate is then steady and low, which Opus encodes cheaply, while the audible effect reads as natural dropout rather than a switch.

2.6 Look-ahead peak limiting

Compressors and AGCs operate on smoothed (RMS / mean-square) descriptors because they shape loudness. Limiters protect against peaks, which are exactly the outliers that smoothing hides; therefore a limiter must work sample-by-sample on |x| with a look-ahead delay line so the gain reduction is already in place when the peak arrives:

p[n] = max(|x[n]|, β p[n−1]),   g[n] = min(1, C / p[n]),   y[n] = g[n] · x[n−D]

with ceiling C = 10−1/20 ≈ 0.891 (−1 dBFS), look-ahead D = 64 samples (4 ms at 16 kHz), release β = e−1/(0.08 fs) (80 ms). This guarantees |y| ≤ C on every sample while keeping the gain envelope inaudible. The stakeholder question — "individual samples or RMS?" — is answered: samples, because the failure mode being prevented (clipping into the codec) is a per-sample phenomenon; RMS limiting would let isolated transients through (design argument #3).

2.7 Chain ordering — the central architectural argument

The original instinct of the product team was threshold first, then normalise. Analysis showed this inverts the correct order:

  • Normalising after gating measures a chopped signal: 400 ms windows fill with zeros and the gain servo hunts.
  • The gate threshold then has to track both noise-floor drift and talker distance, compounding two unknowns.
  • Applying gain after a hard gate re-introduces discontinuities post-gain.

The adopted topology is a sidechain split: measurement (K-weighted energy, VAD) taps the signal before any gain, while gain and gate are applied in series on the audio path, and the VAD freezes the AGC update rather than muting the audio. In one sentence: measure pre-gain, apply pre-gate, update only on speech. The limiter sits last because its contract (|y| ≤ C) must hold for the final signal only.
