7.4 Discussion

  • Hold leak vs. chopping is a genuine trade-off. The stakeholder explicitly requested longer hold; the measured price is ≈ 1 s of background (seg3 +8.8 dB, seg6 +14.6 dB vs. input) after each utterance. Both knobs are single constants (hangTime, holdExtraTime) and can be dialled per product taste; a DNN VAD (§2.4) is the principled way to shrink the leak without reintroducing chopping.
  • Loud-speech target overshoot (−14 vs −19). The servo error uses K-weighted 32 ms RMS while the table reports broadband segment RMS; the two differ by several dB on speech-like spectra, and the 1.5 s release never fully settles inside a 2 s burst. For a communication channel this lands louder than target — safe (limiter guarantees no clip) but worth calibrating against a K-weighted measurement when the chain is ported to SeaTime.
  • Quiet-speech AGC ride is slow by design. seg1 passes at unity because the release time constant exceeds the burst length. Long talkers benefit; two-second asides do not. A dual-rate release (fast catch when error > 10 dB, slow otherwise) is the standard remedy — future work.
  • Measurement trust. The single largest schedule risk was not DSP knowledge but instrument validity (M7). The final process invariant — every claim needs a number from a decoder whose format handling has been verified against a header dump — is now part of the bench.
