8. Conclusions and Future Work

8.1 Conclusions

  1. The product requirement is met. Both manual sliders can be removed: an adaptive min-statistics VAD (9/4 dB hysteresis, 600 ms hold, flatness exit) replaces the threshold, and a VAD-frozen AGC (−19 dBFS target, bounded ±gain, asymmetric ballistics) replaces the gain, with a per-sample limiter guaranteeing ≤ −1 dBFS into Opus.
  2. The theoretical shortcuts held under test. Re-deriving BS.1770 K-weighting at runtime instead of porting 48 kHz coefficients, using R128's perception model but not its time constants, and replacing the contested bimodality heuristic with cap-booted lower-only min-statistics were all vindicated by the final measurements — while the stakeholder's fear (threshold heuristics are fragile) was also vindicated, in our own first implementation (M3).
  3. Ordering matters more than algorithms. Measure pre-gain, apply pre-gate, update only on speech resolved the contested architecture question and is the design's central transferable idea.
  4. Process, not just code. The probe-bisect methodology with input-independent control probes separated three bug classes — language mistakes, DSP defects, tooling defects — and recovered a schedule that a listening-only process would have lost inside the first hour (M7).
  5. Deliverables are runnable. SeaMicLive.cmajorpatch runs on real WASAPI hardware at 16 kHz today; the offline bench reproduces every number in this report from a single command sequence (Appendix B).
